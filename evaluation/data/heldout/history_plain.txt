So today I want to talk about the Industrial Revolution, which began in Britain in the late eighteenth century. The Industrial Revolution was a period in which production shifted from hand tools to machines and from farms to factories. Several conditions made Britain the starting point. The Agricultural Revolution raised crop yields, which allowed fewer farmers to feed a growing population. Many displaced rural workers moved to towns and became a labor force for factories. Britain also had large deposits of coal and iron, and its colonies supplied raw materials such as cotton. Finally, a stable government and a strong banking system encouraged investment in new technology. The key technologies came first in the textile industry. The spinning jenny, invented by James Hargreaves, allowed one worker to spin several threads at once. The steam engine, improved by James Watt, freed factories from dependence on water power. Steam power then drove locomotives, and the railway network lowered the cost of moving goods and people. Industrialization changed society as well. Rapid urbanization produced crowded cities with poor sanitation, and diseases spread quickly. Factory owners often employed women and children for long hours and low wages. These conditions led workers to form trade unions, which campaigned for better pay and safer workplaces. Reformers also pushed Parliament to pass the Factory Acts, which limited working hours for children. Over time the growth of industry produced a new middle class of merchants and factory owners, while the working class gained political influence through the expansion of voting rights.
